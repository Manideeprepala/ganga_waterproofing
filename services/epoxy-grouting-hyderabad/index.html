Why tile joints leak

Ordinary cement grout is porous. Within a few years it absorbs water, cracks, crumbles and collects dirt, and every open joint lets water pass under the tiles into the slab below. In bathrooms and balconies this is one of the most common causes of leakage into the flat below.

Epoxy grouting in Hyderabad replaces that cement grout with a two-part epoxy that is non-absorbent and chemically resistant. It is the go-to fix for bathroom leakage without breaking tiles and for balcony waterproofing, and it keeps kitchen and floor joints clean for years.

When epoxy grouting is the right fix

  • Leak through tile jointsDamp below a bathroom or balcony where the tiles themselves are firmly fixed.
  • Black, stained groutJoints that stay dark and cannot be cleaned.
  • Crumbling or missing groutJoints washing out, especially in showers and wash areas.
  • Fungus in the jointsMould growing in bathroom grout lines.
  • Kitchen counter & floor jointsOil and food stains soaking into grout lines.
  • New tiling workThe best time to use epoxy grout is when tiles are first laid.

Our epoxy grouting process

  1. Check the tiles

    We confirm the tiles are firmly bonded; hollow tiles are re-fixed first.

  2. Rake out old grout

    Old cement grout is removed to a proper depth with a grout-cutting tool, without damaging the tile edges.

  3. Clean & dry the joints

    Joints are vacuumed and dried so the epoxy bonds fully.

  4. Fill with epoxy grout

    Two-part epoxy grout is mixed and pressed into the joints, then the tile surface is cleaned before it sets.

  5. Cure

    The grout is left to cure before the area is used, usually by the next day.

Where we use epoxy grouting

Bathrooms & shower areas

Stops leaks through floor and wall tile joints into the flat below.

Balconies & utility areas

Waterproof joints that survive sun, rain and daily mopping.

Kitchen counters & floors

Stain-proof joints that do not absorb oil and food.

Swimming pools & water features

Chemical-resistant joints for permanently wet areas.
