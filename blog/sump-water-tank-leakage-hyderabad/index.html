Sump and Water Tank Leakage: Causes, Signs and Repair

Most Hyderabad homes store water in an underground sump or an overhead tank. When that tank leaks, you lose water every day and dirty groundwater can find its way in. Here is how to spot a leaking sump, why it happens, and how it is waterproofed, with a recent job from Suncity.

By Ganga Waterproofing Services · 24 October 2026

Why a leaking sump is a bigger problem than it looks

An underground sump sits in the soil, out of sight. A small crack can leak for months before anyone notices. Stored water is lost every day, the motor runs longer to keep the tank full, and in the monsoon, when the ground around the sump is wet, water can flow the other way: soil water and seepage can enter the tank through the same cracks. Over time the moisture also attacks the concrete and the steel inside it.

Signs your sump or water tank is leaking

  • The water level drops even when nobody is using water and the inlet is closed.
  • The motor runs more often than it used to, or the water bill goes up for no reason.
  • The ground around the sump stays wet, or there is seepage on nearby walls and floors.
  • Muddy or discoloured water in the tank after rain.
  • Visible cracks or damp patches on the inside walls when the tank is empty.
  • Moss or algae growing on the outside of an overhead tank.

A simple check: fill the sump, close the inlet, switch off the motor and mark the water level. If it drops noticeably over a few hours with no water being used, the tank is leaking.

Common causes of sump and tank leakage

  • Cracks in the walls or floor from settlement, poor curing or age.
  • Joints between the floor and walls, and between the walls and the slab on top.
  • Pipe openings where the inlet, outlet and overflow pipes pass through the wall.
  • No waterproofing at construction, or old plaster that has become porous.

How sump waterproofing is done

  1. Inspection. We check the tank, the level drop and the area around it to find where it is leaking.
  2. Empty and clean. The tank is emptied, the silt is removed and the walls are cleaned so materials can bond.
  3. Treat cracks and joints. Cracks are opened and filled, and the floor-wall joints and pipe openings are sealed.
  4. Waterproof the walls and floor with a system chosen for the tank's condition.
  5. Cure and test. The tank is given time to cure, then filled and checked before handover.
